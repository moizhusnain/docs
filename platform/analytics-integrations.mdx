The AI CMO runs a full SEO audit when you set up your project, but that audit is a snapshot. To understand what is actually happening on your site day to day, you need live data: who is visiting, where they are coming from, which pages they land on, what they search for, and how your rankings are moving. That is what Google Analytics and Google Search Console give you.

Both are free. Connecting them takes a few minutes and you only do it once.

### **Why Connect Them**

Without these connections, the AI CMO works from the SEO audit alone. It can tell you what is technically broken or under-optimised, but it cannot tell you whether traffic is growing, which content is working, or what your audience is actually searching for.

Once you connect Google Analytics, the CMO knows your real traffic numbers: sessions, users, engagement, what sources are sending visitors, and how that compares to last week or last month.

Once you connect Search Console, it knows your keyword footprint: which queries people use to find you, how many clicks and impressions each generates, and where your average ranking position sits.

With both live, the CMO can give you answers grounded in real evidence rather than static audit data.

### **Connecting Google Analytics**

**Step 1: Open the connection prompt**

Go to your CMO project. In the Analytics panel on the right side of the dashboard, click the link icon in the header. Alternatively, go to Settings and open the Integrations tab. Under Analytics, you will see the Google Analytics option.

**Step 2: Authorise via Google**

Click **Google Analytics**. You will be taken to Google to sign in and authorise the connection. Once done, you are brought back to Okara automatically.

**Step 3: Select a property**

After connecting, you will be asked to choose which Google Analytics 4 property to track. If your Google account manages multiple properties, they will all appear in a dropdown. Select the one for this site and click **Connect Property**.

That is all the setup. The Traffic tab in your Analytics panel will populate with your data.

### **What Google Analytics Shows You**

Once connected, the Traffic tab shows two view options: last 7 days and last 30 days. Each metric card shows the current period value alongside the change versus the equivalent previous period.

The six metric cards are:

- **Active Users**: people who had at least one engaged session or triggered a key event in the period
- **Sessions**: total browsing sessions started, one user can have multiple sessions
- **Engagement Rate**: percentage of sessions that lasted over 10 seconds, had a key event, or had two or more page views
- **Returning**: users who visited before and came back, calculated as total users minus new users
- **Key Events**: conversion actions you defined in GA4, such as sign-ups or purchases
- **Bounce Rate**: percentage of sessions where the user left after one page with no interaction, lower is better

Below the cards, a line chart shows daily active users across the selected period so you can see trends rather than just totals.

**Traffic by Channel**

A channel breakdown shows how users found your site: Organic Search, Direct, Referral, Social, Email, Paid Search, and others. Each channel shows its share as a percentage bar relative to the top channel. This is your first-touch attribution, what drove someone to visit for the first time.

**Top Sources**

Below the channel breakdown, Top Sources shows the specific referring domains and mediums sending traffic. Where the channel view shows "Organic Social" as a category, Sources shows the actual platform or referring domain, like [t.co](http://t.co) or newsletter.

**Top Landing Pages**

This shows which pages users entered the site on, ranked by active users. A page that appears here but has a high bounce rate or low engagement is a conversion problem, not a traffic problem. The CMO can help you think through what to do about it.

**Device Breakdown**

Splits sessions across Desktop, Mobile, and Tablet. If most of your traffic is mobile but your mobile performance score in the Health tab is weak, that is a meaningful problem the CMO can help you prioritize.

### **Connecting Google Search Console**

**Step 1: Open the connection prompt**

Same path as Google Analytics: the link icon in the Analytics panel header, or Settings → Integrations → Analytics.

**Step 2: Authorise via Google**

Click **Google Search Console**. You will be taken to Google for OAuth authorisation and then returned automatically.

**Step 3: Select a site**

Your verified Search Console properties will appear in a dropdown. Select the one matching this CMO project. If no verified sites appear, you need to verify ownership in Google Search Console first before the connection will work. Click **Connect Property** once you have made a selection.

The data will begin syncing and update daily from then on.

### **What Google Search Console Shows You**

The Search Console section of the Traffic tab has four metric cards with 7-day and 30-day views:

- **Total Clicks**: clicks from Google Search results to your site in the period
- **Impressions**: times your pages appeared in Google Search results, whether clicked or not
- **Avg. CTR**: click-through rate, clicks divided by impressions. A low CTR with high impressions means your titles or descriptions are not compelling enough relative to where you rank
- **Avg. Position**: your average ranking position across all queries. Position 1 is the top result. Lower numbers are better and an improving trend here compounds over time

**Daily Performance Chart**

Below the cards, a line chart plots daily data for the period. You can switch between Clicks, Impressions, CTR, and Position to see trends on each metric independently. Hover over any point to see the exact daily value.

**Top Queries**

The queries table shows the search terms that drove the most clicks to your site. Each row shows the query, clicks, and impressions. Queries with high impressions but low clicks are candidates for title or meta description improvements. The CMO can help you identify those and write better copy.

**Top Countries**

A country breakdown shows which countries are driving the most clicks, with a relative bar chart. Useful if you are considering language or regional content investments.

**Top Pages**

Which specific pages are getting organic clicks and impressions. Combined with the Top Landing Pages from Google Analytics, this tells you which pages are performing organically and whether that organic traffic is converting once people arrive.

### **Asking the CMO About Your Data**

Once both connections are live, you can ask the AI CMO questions in chat and it will read directly from your analytics data to answer.

<Callout icon="pencil">
  Which search queries should I be writing more content about?
</Callout>

<Callout icon="pencil">
  My organic traffic dropped this month. What does the data show?
</Callout>

<Callout icon="pencil">
  What are my most visited pages and how is their engagement?
</Callout>

<Callout icon="pencil">
  Which channel is sending me the most engaged users?
</Callout>

The CMO pulls from both the Google Analytics and Search Console results stored for your project. If a connection is missing or data has not synced yet, it will tell you clearly and explain what to connect. If the data is there, every answer it gives you is grounded in the actual numbers for your site.
