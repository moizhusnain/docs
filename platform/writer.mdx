Content is one of the most reliable ways to grow your organic reach. The problem is it takes time, researching keywords, writing drafts, editing, publishing. The Articles agent handles all of that for you. This tutorial walks you through how it works and how to get your first article live.

### **Why Articles Matter**

Every article you publish is a signal to search engines that your site is active, relevant, and worth surfacing. Over time, a consistent stream of quality content builds your domain authority and brings in readers who are already searching for what you offer.

The CMO does not just generate random content. It works with the SEO agent to identify real keyword opportunities where you have a genuine chance to rank. It looks at what your competitors, what your audience is looking for, and what gaps exist that you could fill. Each article in your Feed comes out of that research.

### **Step 1: Open an Article and Review It**

Head to your Feed. You will see article suggestions listed alongside your other tasks. Click on any article to open the full draft.

Inside, you will find the full piece; headline, body, structure. Read through it. The CMO has already written this around a specific keyword and intent, but you know your business better than any agent does.

If something needs changing, **ask the CMO to revise it.** Click "Ask CMO" on the top bar of Tell the CMO what you want changed, a different angle, a shorter intro, a more direct conclusion and it will rewrite based on your direction.

### **Step 2: Connect Your Site**

Before you can publish, you need to connect your website. Click the publish button, from the dropdown, select your hosting service. Okara connects directly with Framer, Webflow, WordPress (self-hosted), and [WordPress.com](http://WordPress.com). You can also go to [okara.ai/settings](https://okara.ai/settings) to manage your integrations. You only do this once, and from that point on publishing is a single click.

Here is how to connect different providers

**Framer**

To connect Framer, you need two things: your project URL and an API key.

Finding your API key in Framer:

1. Open your project in Framer.
2. Go to your project settings.
3. Look for the CMS or API section and generate an API key there.
4. Copy the key.

Back in Okara, paste your project URL and the API key into the Framer connection fields and save.

**Webflow**

To connect Webflow, you need to generate an API token from your site settings.

1. Open your Webflow project.
2. Go to Site Settings.
3. Click Apps and Integrations.
4. Select API Access.
5. Click Generate API Token and copy it.

Paste the token into the Webflow connection field in Okara and save.

**Wordpress Self Hosted**

For self-hosted WordPress, you need your site URL, your username, and an application password. Application passwords are different from your login password -- here is how to create one.

1. Log in to your WordPress admin dashboard.
2. Go to **Users** and click **Profile**.
3. Scroll down to **Application Passwords**.
4. Enter a name for the password (something like "Okara") and click **Add New Application Password**.
5. WordPress will show you the password once. Copy it immediately -- you will not see it again.

In Okara, enter your site URL, your WordPress username, and the application password you just created.

**WordPress.com**

For [WordPress.com](http://WordPress.com) sites, there is no manual setup. Click the WordPress.com option in your Okara integrations and follow the OAuth flow. You will be taken to WordPress.com to authorize the connection and then brought back to Okara automatically.

Here is what you can set:

- **Title**: The CMO will have already populated this; you can edit it here if you want.
- **URL slug**: This is also pre-filled based on the title, but you can change it.
- **Collection**: select the blog collection or content type you want the article to publish into.
- **Status**: Choose whether the article goes live as a published post or saves as a draft for you to review in your CMS first.
- **Auto-publishing**: Set this to on if you want future articles to publish automatically, or leave it off to keep approving them one by one.

### Step 4: Set Up Auto-Publishing

Auto-publishing is the setting that lets the CMO maintain a steady content output without you having to approve every single article.

You can toggle this on in the publish panel at the point of publishing.

A good approach is to keep it off for the first few articles. Read through them, get comfortable with the quality and tone, and make sure the output sounds like you. Once you are happy, turning auto-publishing on means your content pipeline keeps moving in the background while you focus on everything else.

That is all it takes. Find an article in your Feed, review or edit it, connect your site, configure your publish settings, and go. The CMO keeps surfacing new opportunities as long as your site is connected, so your content pipeline stays full without you having to think about it.
