The AI CMO is not just a background agent running on a schedule. It is also a chat assistant you can talk to directly, ask to write specific content, and tune to match your voice. This guide walks through how the chat works, what you can ask it to do, and how to use Writing Instructions to make sure everything it produces sounds like you.

### **What the AI CMO Does on Its Own**

Before getting into the chat, it helps to understand what the CMO is already doing without you. Every day it works through a set of tasks automatically.

It surfaces two SEO and GEO issues from your audit for you to review and fix. It writes one long-form article built around a keyword opportunity identified for your site. It finds two Reddit threads where a reply from your brand would be genuinely useful. And it drafts one tweet and linkedin post ready for you to review.

### **Talking to the AI CMO**

Open the chat from the CMO dashboard. You can ask it anything related to your growth: questions about your SEO report, requests to write a new post, or help understanding a recommendation.

The CMO has full context about your product, your competitors, and your content history. You do not need to explain your business every time you open a conversation. It remembers what matters across sessions.

### **Using Intentions to Focus the CMO**

The fastest way to get useful output is to tell the CMO which area you are working in. The @ button in the chat input opens a picker where you can select a focus: Company, Reddit, SEO & GEO, X Writer, Articles, Hacker News, or LinkedIn.

Selecting one routes the CMO directly to the right tools without any back-and-forth. If you pick **X Writer**, it brings your tweet suggestions in context immediately. If you pick **Articles**, it loads your existing drafts and is ready to help you refine or create new ones.

### **Writing Content Through Chat**

**Articles**

To write a new article, tell the CMO what you want it to cover. Be as specific or as loose as you like.

<Callout icon="pencil">
  Write an article about why B2B SaaS companies underinvest in content at the early stage.
</Callout>

The CMO will search the web for current context, pull in your product documents to ground the piece in your positioning, and then write the full draft. The article is saved to your dashboard automatically.

If you want to refine something it already generated, open the @Articles intention and ask it to rewrite a specific piece. You can give it direction: tighten the intro, change the angle, make the conclusion more actionable.

The new generated article will be added to your action feed and you can publish it directly to your site.

**X Posts**

Ask the CMO for a new tweet by describing the idea.

<Callout icon="pencil">
  Write a tweet about the hidden cost of ignoring technical SEO.
</Callout>

It will produce a ready-to-post draft. If you want options, ask for several.

<Callout icon="pencil">
  Give me three different angles on that.
</Callout>

Each version is saved to your X task feed so you can post directly from there. The CMO will not generate a tweet in plain text and call it done. It always goes through the writing tool, which applies your saved platform instructions and formats the output correctly.

**LinkedIn Posts**

LinkedIn post generation works the same way. Ask for a post on a topic and the CMO writes it from scratch, grounded in your product context.

<Callout icon="pencil">
  Write a LinkedIn post about how we helped a client cut their SEO audit backlog in half.
</Callout>

If you want multiple options for the same idea, ask for them.

<Info>
  Give me three versions of that.
</Info>

Like with X, posts are saved directly to your LinkedIn feed when generated through chat.

**Reddit Replies**

The CMO also surfaces Reddit threads where your brand could add genuine value. If you see one in your Feed and want a custom reply, you can ask the CMO directly.

<Info>
  Write a reply for this Reddit post.
</Info>

Paste the link or reference the post from your Feed. The CMO reads the thread, identifies what the community needs, and drafts a reply that helps without being promotional.

**Attaching Files**

You can upload files directly in the chat. Paste an image, drop in a PDF, or attach a CSV, Word document, or spreadsheet. The CMO will read it and use it to inform whatever you are working on.

This is useful when you want the CMO to write content based on a brief, a research document, or a style guide you have prepared.

### **Tuning the CMO with Writing Instructions**

The most valuable thing you can do after generating your first few pieces of content is to save Writing Instructions for each platform. This is how you make the CMO write the way you want, consistently, without having to repeat yourself every conversation.

Writing Instructions tell the CMO how to write for a specific platform. Not what to write about, but how. Tone, format, length, things to avoid, voice, structure. Once saved, every piece of content the CMO generates for that platform follows those rules automatically.

To open the writing instructions, click on the settings button on the top right corner of your action feed.

Good Writing Instructions are specific and practical. A few things worth capturing:

- Tone: formal, conversational, blunt, warm
- Format: bullet points or prose, long or short, with or without headers
- Things to avoid: corporate buzzwords, em dashes, excessive hedging, emoji
- Call to action style: direct ask, soft close, question at the end
- Audience assumptions: what the reader already knows, what they care about

You can set separate instructions for each platform. Your X voice might be shorter and punchier than your LinkedIn voice. The CMO keeps them separate and applies the right one automatically.

The chat is where you shape the CMO's output and direct it toward the work that matters most right now. The autonomous daily tasks keep the pipeline moving, but the chat is where you go deeper, course-correct, and make sure everything coming out sounds like your brand.
