Up to this point in your Okara journey, you have probably noticed that SEO and GEO fixes show up consistently in your Feed. There is a good reason for that. These are the structural improvements that make your site visible. They are what help Google, Bing, and AI-powered search tools actually find and understand your content. Without them, even the best websites are working harder than they need to.

SEO is not a one-time project. It is the foundation that everything else builds on. The articles you publish, the backlinks you earn, the traffic you drive from social; all of it performs better when your site is technically sound. And the work compounds over time. A fix you make today keeps paying off months from now, even when you are not thinking about it.

### The Problem Everyone Knows About but Nobody Fixes

Here is the reality: fixing SEO issues usually means editing code. Updating meta tags, adding structured data, fixing broken canonical URLs, improving page speed, restructuring headings -- these are not things you can do from a dashboard. They require changes to your actual codebase.

And that is where most SEO fixes go to die.

It is not that anyone thinks they are unimportant. The opposite, actually. Everyone agrees they matter. But when you look at your roadmap, there are always product features to ship, bugs to squash, customer requests to handle. SEO fixes are important but rarely urgent, which means they keep sliding to the bottom of the list. Week after week. Month after month.

This is what makes it a silent growth killer. You do not feel the pain of a missing meta description the way you feel the pain of a broken checkout flow. But over time, those unfixed issues add up. Your rankings slip. Your competitors, who did make those fixes, start showing up where you used to. And because the decline is gradual, you might not even notice it until you have lost real ground.

SEO works for you around the clock. It brings in visitors while you sleep, while you are in meetings, while you are on vacation. But only if the foundation is solid. Every unfixed issue is a small crack in that foundation.

### The Coding Agent: Let the CMO Fix It for You

This is exactly the problem we built the Coding Agent to solve. Instead of adding SEO fixes to your engineering backlog and hoping they get prioritized, you can now let the CMO handle the code changes directly.

The Coding Agent is a cloud-based developer that works inside your actual codebase. When you tell it to fix something, it reads your code, understands the structure, makes the necessary changes, and opens a pull request for you to review. You do not need to write anything yourself. You just review the work and merge it.

### Connect Your GitHub Repository

Before the Coding Agent can work on your code, it needs access to your repository.

1. Go to [okara.ai/settings](https://okara.ai/settings).
2. Open the **Integrations** section.
3. Find GitHub and click **Connect**.
4. You will be taken through the GitHub authorization flow. Select the repository (or repositories) you want Okara to have access to and confirm.

That is it. Once your repo is connected, the Coding Agent can start working on fixes whenever you ask it to.

### Let the Agent Handle It

Open any SEO or GEO fix. You will see the usual detail view that explains what the issue is and why it matters. But now you will also see a new option: **Fix with Coding Agent**.

Click it.

The Coding Agent spins up in the cloud. It pulls down your codebase and It makes the changes. The agent writes code that fits your existing patterns and conventions. Finally, It opens a pull request on your GitHub repository with a clear description of what was changed and why.

The whole process runs in the background. You do not need to stay on the page. 

The Coding Agent does the work, but you stay in control. Every change comes through as a pull request, which means you (or your engineering team) can review it the same way you would review any other code change.

### Why This Changes the Game

With the Coding Agent, that entire cycle collapses to minutes. You see a fix in your Feed, click a button, review a PR, and merge. Done.

That means the fixes that used to get buried under your roadmap now actually get shipped. Your site gets stronger with every merge. Your rankings improve. And the compounding effect of consistent technical SEO starts working in your favour instead of against you.
