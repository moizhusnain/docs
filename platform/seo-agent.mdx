Search engine optimization is not a one-time task. It is an ongoing process of finding what is broken, fixing it, and building on what is already working. The AI CMO handles the research and monitoring, but knowing how to read the data makes you a better collaborator. This guide walks through the Analytics panel, explains what each tab tells you, and shows how the SEO and GEO recommendations in your Feed connect to everything you see here.

### **Why SEO and GEO Are Different Things**

SEO is optimising for traditional search engines: Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo. The goal is to rank in the list of blue links when someone searches for something you offer.

GEO is Generative Engine Optimisation. It is the newer challenge of making sure your content gets cited and summarised correctly by AI-powered tools: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and similar. These tools do not just rank pages. They read them, pull out the key points, and surface them directly to users. If your content is not structured well, they will either skip it or misrepresent it.

The CMO audits both. The Analytics panel shows you where you stand on each.

### **The Analytics Panel**

The Analytics panel lives on your CMO dashboard. It pulls data from the full SEO audit that ran when you set up your project, and updates as the CMO runs new audits. There are six tabs: Traffic, Health, Links, Technical, AI/GEO, and Checks.

### **Traffic**

The Traffic tab shows data from Google Analytics and Google Search Console once you connect them. It is the first tab you see once those integrations are live. This tab is only accessible when the integrations are connected and live.

Google Analytics shows you how people are using your site: sessions, users, engagement rates, and traffic trends over time. Google Search Console shows the search-side picture: which queries are bringing people in, how many clicks and impressions you are getting, and where your average positions sit.

To connect either service, click the link icon in the Analytics panel header, or go to Settings and open the Integrations tab. The connection uses Google OAuth, so you will be taken to Google to authorise it and then brought back automatically.

Once connected, you will need to select a property in Analytics and a site in Search Console. The CMO walks you through that on the first visit after connecting.

### **Health**

The Health tab is your page performance dashboard. It shows scores from a live audit across four categories for both mobile and desktop: Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices, and SEO.

Each score is a number from 0 to 100. The SEO score reflects on-page signals like meta tags, headings, and structured data. The Performance score is about speed and loading behaviour. These are distinct measurements, so a high overall SEO score does not guarantee fast load times, and vice versa.

Below the score rings you will see Core Web Vitals for mobile. These are the specific timing metrics Google uses to assess page experience:

- **LCP (Largest Contentful Paint)**: how long it takes for the main content to appear. Under 2.5 seconds is good.
- **FID / INP (Interaction to Next Paint)**: how quickly the page responds to user input.
- **CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift)**: how much the page layout jumps around while loading. Lower is better.

Further down, the Health tab shows your meta tags, heading structure, image alt text coverage, and link counts. These are the technical foundations that search engines rely on to understand what your page is about.

### **Links**

The Links tab covers your backlink profile. Backlinks are links pointing to your site from other websites. They are one of the strongest signals search engines use to decide how authoritative your domain is.

The tab shows your total backlink count, referring domains, referring IPs, and spam score. Referring domains matter more than raw backlink count. One link from a single trusted domain is worth more than ten links from the same domain.

Your spam score is worth watching. A high spam score means a significant portion of your backlinks come from low-quality or suspicious sources. If that number is high, the CMO will surface it.

You will also see broken backlinks and broken pages. Broken backlinks are links from other sites pointing to pages that no longer exist on your domain. Each one is a lost signal. The CMO flags them so you can decide whether to redirect or recover the page.

The full backlinks breakdown, including link type distribution and top referring domains, is available on the Max plan.

### **Technical**

The Technical tab goes deeper than the Health tab into how your page is built and served. It pulls on-page data from the SEO audit and shows things like:

- **Page timing**: how long different parts of the load process take
- **Render-blocking resources**: scripts or stylesheets that delay the page from displaying
- **Content relevance metrics**: keyword signals the crawlers found
- **Heading structure**: the H1, H2, H3 hierarchy used on the page
- **Social tags**: Open Graph and Twitter Card metadata used when your page is shared
- **Resource errors**: broken images, scripts, or stylesheets the crawler detected

Most of the issues surfaced here will also appear as specific recommendations in your Feed. The Technical tab is the raw data view. Your Feed is where the CMO translates that into prioritised fixes you can act on.

### **AI/GEO**

The AI/GEO tab answers the question: how well is your site set up to be found and cited by AI tools?

It gives you an AI Readiness Score from 0 to 100, calculated from a checklist of ten signals. Each check gets a pass or fail, and the score is the percentage of checks passed.

The ten checks are:

- **Structured Data ([Schema.org](http://Schema.org))**: machine-readable markup that helps LLMs understand your content type, author, date, and other attributes
- **Meta Description**: a clear, descriptive summary that LLMs use when synthesising your page
- **Clear Heading Structure**: proper H1 tags so crawlers can parse the document hierarchy
- **Sufficient Content Depth**: enough words on the page to be worth citing (300 words is the baseline)
- **Canonical URL**: prevents confusion when multiple versions of the same page exist
- **robots.txt**: tells AI crawlers what they are and are not allowed to access
- **llms.txt**: a newer convention that explicitly guides LLM crawlers about your site content
- **sitemap.xml**: helps all crawlers discover your full content inventory
- **Language Attribute**: tells multilingual AI systems which language your content is in
- **Content Readability**: a readability grade based on sentence structure and vocabulary complexity

Hover over any check to see the specific detail: what the CMO found, and why it matters.

Below the checklist, the Site Files section shows the exact status of your robots.txt, llms.txt, and sitemap.xml, including file size and a preview of the content.

If your AI Readiness Score is low, the most impactful fixes are usually structured data, llms.txt, and content depth. The CMO will surface the most critical ones in your Feed.

### **Checks**

The Checks tab lists every SEO check that passed during the last audit. It is a confirmation view: you can see what is already correct and does not need attention. Each passed item shows its category and a description of what was verified.

This is useful context when you are deciding what to prioritise. If the list is long, it means the CMO has less to work with in terms of easy wins.

### **SEO and GEO Recommendations in Your Feed**

The Analytics panel shows you the state of your site. Your Feed is where the CMO tells you what to do about it.

Every day the CMO surfaces two SEO and GEO issues for you to review. These come directly from the latest audit, ranked by priority. Critical issues with the highest impact potential appear first.

Each recommendation in the Feed shows the affected URL, the category of the issue (Performance, On-Page, Technical, AI/GEO), and its priority level. Click to open the full detail: what the problem is, why it matters, and a step-by-step implementation guide written for your specific site.

Once you have fixed an issue, mark it as complete in the Feed. The CMO will note it and move on to the next opportunity in the queue.
